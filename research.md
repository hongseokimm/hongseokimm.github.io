<ol class="paper-list">
  <li>
    <span class="entry-title">The Welfare Cost of Social Security Insolvency Risk</span>
    <details class="paper-abstract">
      <summary>Abstract <span class="paper-pdf">· <a href="{{ site.job_market_paper_link | relative_url }}" download>pdf</a></span></summary>
      <p>This paper studies the welfare cost of perceived Social Security insolvency. If households expect future benefit cuts, they may save more, work longer, or claim earlier before any cut occurs. I quantify these distortions in a life-cycle model with endogenous saving, labor supply, and claiming, disciplined by 1995--2025 subjective expectations data and linked to OASI finances. The model measures private welfare losses and fiscal feedbacks, asking whether pessimism is self-validating, self-correcting, or mainly privately costly. The results show that credible communication about payable benefits can have value even without changing taxes or scheduled benefits.</p>

  <li>
    <span class="entry-title">Generosity, Legibility, and Fertility Inertia</span>
    <details class="paper-abstract">
      <summary>Abstract <span class="paper-pdf">· <a href="{{ '/assets/files/Fertility_Inertia_Kim.pdf' | relative_url }}" download>pdf</a></span></summary>
      <p>Governments spend heavily to raise fertility, yet births barely respond, and when they do, what matters is whether a household can verify what it will receive, not how much the program spends. This paper develops a dynastic fertility model that explains why legibility matters as much as generosity. Parents observe public news and policy announcements but cannot verify how strongly either applies to their own household. Evaluating each family plan under its least favorable admissible interpretation, parents exhibit fertility inertia. Over a range of moderately adverse signals, news lowers welfare but leaves fertility, child investment, and consumption unchanged. The same caution prices a transfer at its worst-case household-level pass-through rather than its fiscal size. An illegible program must be scaled up by the reciprocal of that pass-through to match a transparent entitlement, and generosity without legibility widens the inertia region. Family policy should therefore be evaluated on legibility as well as generosity.</p>
    </details>
  </li>
</ol>
